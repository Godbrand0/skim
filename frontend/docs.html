Skim documentation

Withdraw small amounts of MUSD, mUSDC or sats from Mezo as USDC on Base, even when they are below Mezo's own withdrawal minimum.

Overview

Mezo's native bridge has a high minimum for dollar tokens and a fixed fee, so a balance of a few dollars cannot leave Mezo at all. Skim fixes this with a pre-funded pool of USDC on Base, called the float. You send your tokens on Mezo, and the float pays you USDC on Base within a minute or two. Later, the operator moves the collected tokens out of Mezo in one large batch and refills the float. The fixed costs of the native bridge are paid once per batch instead of once per user.

It also works the other way. Deposit USDC on Base and the same kind of float on Mezo pays you mUSDC. When both directions are running, each one helps refill the other (see Architecture).

What it is not

  • It is not Mezo's official bridge and is not operated by Mezo.
  • It is a beta. The contracts have not been audited, and per-transfer and daily limits apply.
  • It is not trustless. Your payout depends on the operator's relayer and float (see Trust and security).

How it works

  1. You deposit on Mezo. You call the deposit contract with your token (or BTC) and the Base address that should receive USDC. The contract forwards your funds to the operator's treasury in the same transaction and emits a Deposit event with a unique id. It never holds your funds.
  2. The relayer waits for confirmation. A service watches Mezo for deposit events and waits for a few blocks (5 by default) before acting.
  3. The relayer prices the deposit. It converts your amount to USD, subtracts the fee, and checks the payout against the float's limits and balance.
  4. The float pays you. The relayer calls release on the float contract on Base, using the deposit id as a single-use reference. USDC arrives at your address.
  5. The operator rebalances later. Collected tokens are swapped to mUSDC on Mezo, withdrawn through Mezo's native bridge to Ethereum, moved to Base with Circle's CCTP, and sent to the float. This batch step is done by the operator and is not part of your transfer.

The other direction: USDC on Base to mUSDC on Mezo

It is the same flow in reverse. You deposit USDC on Base into a deposit contract, which forwards it to a treasury on Base and records the deposit. The relayer waits for 10 Base blocks, prices it at $1.00 for 1 mUSDC, takes the same fee, and pays mUSDC to your Mezo address from a float held on Mezo. The contracts are the same two contracts, deployed the other way round, so a reference can still be paid only once.

One thing to know: Mezo network fees are paid in BTC. A wallet that arrives with only mUSDC and no BTC cannot spend it yet. A small BTC top-up on arrival is on the roadmap.

Using the bridge

The bridge opens in the Mezo to Base direction. Press the round switch button between the two panels to go the other way, from USDC on Base to mUSDC on Mezo. The button only appears once that direction is switched on. Everything below works the same in both directions, with the networks swapped.

  1. Connect your wallet. Any browser wallet that supports custom networks works. If you are on another network, the button changes to Switch to Mezo and adds the network for you if needed (chain ID 31612, gas token BTC).
  2. Pick the token you send. The dropdown lists mUSDC, MUSD and BTC with your balances. For BTC you enter whole sats.
  3. Enter an amount. Max fills your balance, capped at the per-transfer maximum. When you send BTC it leaves a small reserve so you can still pay network fees. The panel on the right shows what you receive, estimated.
  4. Check the Base address. It defaults to your wallet. Use an address you control on Base. Transfers cannot be undone.
  5. Approve, then bridge. For mUSDC and MUSD the first button is Approve, which allows the deposit contract to take exactly the amount you entered, never more. Then press Bridge to Base. BTC needs no approval.
  6. Follow the progress. The tracker shows Sent on Mezo, Confirming on Mezo and USDC sent on Base, with links to both explorers.

Before you send

  • You need a little BTC on Mezo for gas. A deposit costs roughly 0.0000002 BTC, a fraction of a cent. The balance bar warns you when your BTC is very low.
  • The amount shown is an estimate. The relayer sets the final amount when your deposit confirms, using the price at that moment.
  • Sending to the wrong Base address cannot be fixed.

The fee

The fee is a percentage of the value, with a floor so tiny transfers still cover their cost:

fee = max(floor, value x 2%)            default: 2%, floor $0.05
fee += value x 1%   while the float is below 30% of its target   (low-liquidity surcharge)
you receive = value - fee

It covers the operator's cost of moving collected funds out of Mezo in batches (a fixed fee on Mezo's bridge, swaps, and a CCTP hop), the peg and price risk the operator carries, and a small margin.

Why the limits exist

Each deposit has a minimum and maximum enforced by the deposit contract, and the float has three payout caps enforced by the float contract: a maximum per transfer, a maximum per recipient per UTC day, and a maximum for all payouts per UTC day. They keep any single mistake or compromised key small. The defaults at launch are $25 per transfer, $50 per recipient per day and $300 per day, but the operator can raise or lower them.

Trust and security

Skim is a custodial, relayer-based service. It is designed to keep the operator's mistakes and key compromises small, but it does not remove the need to trust the operator.

What you trust the operator for

  • Your deposit goes to the operator's treasury immediately. Nothing on-chain forces the payout to happen. The relayer is expected to pay you.
  • The relayer decides the exact amount you receive, using the rules above.
  • Deposits that cannot be paid automatically are held for manual payout or refund.

What the contracts enforce

  • No double pay. Each deposit id can be paid once. A second release with the same id reverts.
  • Hard caps. The float cannot pay more than its per-transfer, per-recipient and daily limits, even if the relayer key is stolen.
  • Narrow roles. The relayer key can only call release. The guardian can only pause. Only the admin can change limits, unpause or withdraw the float.
  • Pause. Payouts and new deposits can be stopped immediately. The admin can still withdraw the float while paused.
  • Delayed admin handover. Transferring the admin role is two-step with a delay, so a mistake or hijack can be noticed.
  • Deposit contract holds nothing. It forwards funds to the treasury in the same transaction and rejects fee-on-transfer tokens.

Known risks

  • Unaudited code. The contracts have tests and a full local end-to-end test, but no external audit yet. Use small amounts.
  • Liquidity. If the float runs out, payouts wait until it is refilled.
  • Mezo's native bridge. Refills depend on it. A previous vulnerability in its bridge-out path led to a pause while it was fixed, and a pause would delay refills but not stop payouts from the float.
  • Price and peg. BTC and MUSD prices can move between your payout and the operator's conversion. The haircuts and fee absorb normal moves, not extreme ones.
  • Operational. A stopped relayer delays payouts. A wrong Base address cannot be recovered.

Safety properties

  • Idempotent payouts. Before every release the relayer checks refUsed(id) on Base, so a lost database or a restart cannot cause a second payment. Deposits left in sending after a crash are reconciled on startup.
  • Simulate first. Every release is simulated before it is sent, and the relayer starts with payouts disabled.
  • Startup checks. The relayer refuses to start if the RPCs are on the wrong chain, a contract address has no code, or its key does not hold the relayer role.

Rebalancing path

Mezo's native bridge can only withdraw to Ethereum or Bitcoin, not to Base, and requires at least 20 mUSDC per withdrawal with a flat fee of about $3. So each batch is:

BTC  --(BTC/MUSD pool)-->  MUSD  --(MUSD/mUSDC pool)-->  mUSDC
mUSDC --(Mezo native bridge, min 20)--> USDC on Ethereum
USDC on Ethereum --(Circle CCTP)--> USDC on Base --> float

How two directions refill each other

Each direction drains one float and fills one treasury on the other chain:

  • People leaving Mezo take USDC out of the Base float and leave mUSDC in the Mezo treasury.
  • People entering Mezo take mUSDC out of the Mezo float and leave USDC in the Base treasury.

So the USDC piling up in the Base treasury can top up the Base float directly, and the mUSDC piling up in the Mezo treasury can top up the Mezo float directly, with no bridge involved. Only the difference between the two directions ever needs Mezo's bridge, with its minimum and its fee. The operator moves the funds by hand today.

Relayer

The relayer is a TypeScript service for Node 24 using viem and the built-in SQLite. One process runs three loops: it ingests Deposit events, pays pending deposits, and serves the HTTP API.

  • Ingest. Reads Deposit logs from Mezo in chunks of 1,000 blocks, only up to head - CONFIRMATIONS, and stores each in SQLite keyed by deposit id.
  • Price. mUSDC and MUSD use fixed values, BTC uses Mezo's own oracle at 0x7b7c000000000000000000000000000000000015. A price older than one hour or not above zero is rejected, and the deposit waits.
  • Pay. Checks the float state and limits, simulates, sends release, waits for the receipt and records the transaction.
  • Payouts are off by default. The relayer only simulates until ENABLE_PAYOUTS=true.

Operations

These notes are for the operator.

Daily check

Run the monitor from the relayer folder. It prints the float, daily payout use, the 24-hour totals, the queue and the treasury value, and says when to rebalance.

cd relayer && npm run monitor

Rebalancing a batch

Run a batch when the treasury holds at least about $300, or when the float falls below about $90. Keep a batch to about $1,000 or less because the MUSD/mUSDC pool on Mezo is shallow.

  1. On Mezo, swap BTC to MUSD and MUSD to mUSDC in the Mezo app. Keep a little BTC for gas.
  2. Withdraw at least 20 mUSDC to an Ethereum wallet you control (Mezo app, destination Ethereum). The fee is about $3, and it takes roughly 30 to 60 minutes.
  3. Move the USDC from Ethereum to Base with Circle's CCTP, with the float contract as the recipient.
  4. Confirm the float balance rose, and record the batch (amounts, fees, gas).

Mezo's withdrawal chains were Ethereum and Bitcoin only at build time. Re-check the minimum and the supported chains before each batch because they are governance-controlled.

Emergency actions

What to watch

  • The relayer's ETH balance on Base.
  • lastIngestAt in /api/health. If it stops moving, the Mezo RPC or the process is down.
  • Deposits in waiting for more than an hour, and any in manual_review.
  • The MUSD market price against the configured haircut.

Run it yourself

The repository contains the contracts (Foundry), the relayer, this frontend and the deploy scripts. Nothing broadcasts for you: you sign every transaction with your own wallets.

Prerequisites

  • Foundry, Node 24 or newer.
  • An admin multisig, a relayer hot key, a guardian address and a Mezo treasury address. They must all be different.
  • A little ETH on Base for deployment and for the relayer's gas.

Verify first

forge test                                                         # 33 contract tests
BASE_RPC_URL=https://mainnet.base.org forge test --match-path "test/fork/*"
BASE_RPC_URL=https://mainnet.base.org ./scripts/e2e-local.sh        # whole flow on local chains
cd relayer && npm install && npm test && npm run typecheck

Deploy

# Base: dry run, then broadcast with a keystore account
export ADMIN=0x... RELAYER=0x... GUARDIAN=0x...
forge script script/DeployFloat.s.sol --rpc-url https://mainnet.base.org
forge script script/DeployFloat.s.sol --rpc-url https://mainnet.base.org --account deployer --broadcast

# Mezo
export ADMIN=0x... GUARDIAN=0x... TREASURY=0x...
forge script script/DeployDeposit.s.sol --rpc-url $MEZO_RPC_URL
forge script script/DeployDeposit.s.sol --rpc-url $MEZO_RPC_URL --account deployer --broadcast

# then the admin enables the tokens
DEPOSIT=0x... ./scripts/configure-deposit.sh calldata

The deploy scripts refuse to run on the wrong chain and refuse duplicate role addresses. Start with a $50 to $100 float and the relayer in simulation mode, then enable payouts.

Run the relayer and the frontend

cd relayer  && cp .env.example .env && npm install && npm start
cd frontend && cp .env.example .env && npm install && npm run build   # host frontend/dist over HTTPS

The repository's docs/DEPLOY.md has the full checklist, and docs/RUNBOOK.md has the operations steps.

FAQ

Why not just use Mezo's own withdrawal?

It requires at least 20 mUSDC, charges a flat fee of about $3 and only goes to Ethereum. For a $5 balance the fee alone would eat most of it, and the minimum would block it entirely.

Why a percentage fee?

Each batch costs a fixed amount to move out of Mezo, and the operator carries price risk while holding collected funds. A percentage with a small floor spreads those costs across users in proportion to what they move.

How long does it take?

Usually under a minute (a measured transfer took 32 seconds): the relayer waits for 5 Mezo blocks, then sends one transaction on Base. If liquidity is low or payouts are paused, your transfer waits.

My transfer says waiting. Is my money safe?

Yes. Your deposit is recorded on Mezo and in the relayer. It is paid automatically when the cause clears, usually when the float is refilled. The note under the tracker says which cause applies.

What happens if I send the wrong amount or token?

The deposit contract rejects amounts outside the allowed range and tokens that are not enabled, and the transaction reverts so nothing is sent. Only the Base address is unrecoverable if you type the wrong one.

Can I send more than the maximum?

Not in one transfer. Split it into several. Per-recipient and daily limits also apply. Larger amounts are better served by Mezo's own bridge, which has lower proportional fees.

Do I need BTC on Mezo?

Yes, a very small amount, because Mezo gas is paid in BTC. A deposit costs a fraction of a cent.

Is MUSD really worth less than mUSDC here?

MUSD is valued at $0.99 by default because it trades slightly below a dollar and cannot be redeemed for dollars directly. If it moves, the operator adjusts the value.

Can I go from Base to Mezo too?

Yes, once the reverse direction is switched on: deposit USDC on Base and receive mUSDC on Mezo. The switch button between the two panels appears when it is available. The fee and the limits work the same way.

I received mUSDC but have no BTC. What now?

Mezo network fees are paid in BTC, so you need a little BTC on Mezo to move mUSDC. A top-up that delivers a small amount of BTC for gas is planned. Until then, send a small amount of BTC to your address first.

Is this audited?

No. It has automated tests and an end-to-end test, but no independent audit. Treat it as a beta and use amounts you are comfortable with.
